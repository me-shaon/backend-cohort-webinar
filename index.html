09
A hard truth

The Creator Economy
is dead.

For twenty years we worshipped the maker — the coder, the writer, the designer.
Now? Creating is a commodity. Like tap water. Turn the faucet, content pours out.

Everyone can build software now. What took a team of engineers months takes a single afternoon. Building isn't the moat anymore — it's the baseline.

10
The consequence

So where does the value go?

The internet is flooding with “Grey Slop” — content that's technically proficient but spiritually vacant. When everyone can create, the output collapses. Value moves elsewhere.

When access is infinite → value shifts to the filter
When noise is ubiquitous → value shifts to the signal
When creation is cheap → value shifts to curation

Can they cook the same dish?

Why not?

The ingredients were never the secret. The same is true for software — the tools are available to everyone. What separates great engineers is everything else.

The answer

It's not about writing more code.

It's about three things:

What to build — not what they asked for, what they actually need.
Why it matters — the reasoning behind every decision.
How to make it survive — real users, real scale, real failure.
19
The real value

Anyone can build an MVP. Production is a different game.

For one user
Anyone can ship this.
  • Works on your laptop
  • Serves a single request
  • No real concurrency
  • AI can build it in an hour
For thousands of users
Now you need systems thinking.
  • Handle thousands of concurrent users
  • Stay up when things fail
  • Keep data safe and consistent
  • Scale without falling over
  • Make the right trade-offs under pressure

None of that comes from a prompt. It comes from understanding how systems work.

20
Systems thinking in practice
Someone says “Let’s build a real-time chat app.”

You don’t just start coding. You ask:

? Do we need true real-time, or is near-real-time good enough?
? What happens when 10,000 users message at the same time?
? Where does the data live, and who owns it?
? What breaks first when traffic spikes — and how do we handle it?

That’s systems thinking — the difference between something that works and something that lasts.

So, fix the setup

You don’t need permission.
You need a plan and a partner.

01

Write your own plan — today

Pick one roadmap and cut it into eight weeks. Two topics a week, and one small thing you actually build with each. Put it in your calendar like a meeting you can’t move.

  • One roadmap, not five
  • A fixed weekly slot, however small
  • Something shipped every week — however ugly
02

Don’t learn alone

Pair with one friend, or pull three or four colleagues into a group. Same plan, same week, a standing check-in. You show up because someone notices when you don’t.

  • Same schedule, so you stay in sync
  • Take turns teaching the week’s topic
  • Review each other’s code — that’s where it sticks

A plan kills the drift. A partner kills the excuse. That’s two of the four reasons gone.
